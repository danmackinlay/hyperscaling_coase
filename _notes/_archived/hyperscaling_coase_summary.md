## Where the existence test stands

The null fails, and the essay exists, but the optimist's advocate changed what its spine is. Argued as hard as it goes, the null survives on three rows under one condition the table had never written down: that the dials are symmetric across the parties. With equal information about past play, equal commitment reliability and nobody systematically first to move, capable agents converge on the equal split of $1.5$ each, which is the Nash-product maximiser, with no rule supplied: by theorem in the two-party models and by extension on the four-party river, in haggling under recurrent adaptive play (row 3), under short-lived commitments attempted in a random order (row 6), and in patient repeated play (row 9). The same theorems say what an asymmetry does: the bigger sampler of past play, the more reliable committer and the earlier mover take more, and the Nash product falls. Three conditions fail under any reading, symmetric or not, over five rows: sealed unconditional commitment, where the pie is lost with probability $q^2$ (row 5); simultaneous binding declarations under unanimity, where delay grows with reliability (row 8); and divide and conquer at $k=1$ or $2$, where every resident ends below no deal whatever is spent, once (rows 10 and 15) or every year (row 13). And nothing in "give everyone an agent" makes the dials symmetric: one principal against three is first to commit when speed sets the order; a repeat player samples more history than one-shot residents; the side the rules let commit is the side that commits; whoever holds the entitlement sets the threat point. So the essay's spine is now two lists rather than one: the symmetry conditions the agenda presupposes without supplying, and the rows where even symmetry fails together with the rules that repair them. The verdict is the same under Dan's criterion as under every candidate the criterion agent ran (the null fails under the Nash product through rows 5 and 10 alone), and under it rows 1 and 2 also count against compute past a middling budget. **The three symmetric rows go to Dan before anything is drafted**, as the brief required.
