## Complexity theorist (Fable), received first

**Bell cut.** Superadditivity of an α-characteristic function does not make the grand coalition welfare-best in a partition-function game (Thrall–Lucas; Ray–Vohra) [sure]. The Bell term dies anyway because welfare depends only on the public action $x$: every structure yields $0$ or $\pi-\sum h$, and $\{G\}$ attains the max. Keep the cut, change the reason, since "superadditive, hence no search" fails as a method once $v$ depends on the rest of the structure (two firms, reach-dependent hydrology). Also: the plan's $k=0$ cell is not max-min (credits the firm with accepting payment); and Shapley–Shubik is the value of a simple game *without* externalities, while the payoff-relevant game is partition-function form, where the value is not unique (Myerson 1977; Macho-Stadler–Pérez-Castrillo–Wettstein 2007; de Clippel–Serrano 2008) [sure they exist]. The $2^n$ hardness is attached to an object that is not the object bargained over.

**C5.** Nisan–Segal is for private-goods combinatorial allocation. The river's clause problem is a public decision over $2^m$ configurations (combinatorial public project, Papadimitriou–Schapira–Singer 2008; Segal 2007). The exponential survives for *arbitrary* valuations by set-disjointness, $\Omega(2^m)$ even randomized at $n=2$ [sure]. But residents' valuations are $h_i(y)=g_i(e_i(y))$, a private dose-response of few parameters composed with a public exposure map; private information is $O(1)$ in $m$, clause interactions live inside $e_i$ (physics, publishable), and the $2^m$ is operations inside one datacentre, not bits between minds. The essay has bits and operations swapped. Hyper-granular contracts are finer *additive* meshes, $O(m)$. A human cannot hold $2^{50}$ values, so by the essay's own "fixed biological hardware" every human valuation is compressible and the communication requirement is bounded independent of $m$. Narrowing that saves a version: "for valuations with dense Möbius expansion over clauses, $\Omega(2^m)$", then admit no argument gives outfall preferences dense dividends. The strongest surviving complexity-flavoured fact is the *price of truthfulness* (PSS 2008: truthful public-project mechanisms better than $\sqrt m$ need exponential communication), which is $L$ not $D$. Settle by: exhibit one natural outfall valuation with non-sparse dividends not factoring through low-dimensional exposure.

**W4.** Right citations for item valuations: $k$-wise dependent (Conitzer–Sandholm–Santi 2005), hypergraph (Abraham–Babaioff–Dughmi–Roughgarden 2012), MPH-$k$ (Feige et al. 2015); elicitation polynomial for structured classes (Zinkevich–Blum–Sandholm 2003; Blum et al. 2004) [sure]. MC-nets are over agents, not items; the essay cites them for the wrong object. Real interactions are physical (factor through exposure), legal (parsing, what LLMs do), or strategic (Tirole 2009, a signalling story, hence floor). The literature on "how big is a contract" the essay does not cite: Tirole 2009; Anderlini–Felli 1994, 1999; Battigalli–Maggi 2002 [sure]. Spectrum is the one domain with dense bundle valuations, and its institutions cap the bidding language, evidence for C6 and against C5.

**C8.** #P-completeness holds (Deng–Papadimitriou; Matsui–Matsui; Prasad–Kelly) [sure]. The size claim is numerically wrong: the cited DP at $n=50$, cents, is $\approx6\times10^{12}$ ops, cents of compute; meet-in-the-middle $O(n^2 2^{n/2})$ (Uno 2012 [likely]) is $\approx10^{11}$, under a minute; one random permutation samples every player's marginal at $O(n)$, error $\le\tfrac12\sqrt{1/N}$ (Castro 2009; Bachrach et al. 2010). Money error per resident at today's $p$: about \$35 on a billion-dollar bargain; the firm's pick-off prize $\approx10^{-6}W$. **The prize is endogenous to $p$, scaling as $\sqrt p$, so uniform halving shrinks it by $\sqrt2$: C9 and T-ii fail for the only contest the essay instantiates.** The firm's returns have a knee at the resident's precision, so the pivotality race is a decision problem with an opponent-set knee, misfiled as a contest. The river's own $v$ depends on (size or weight, harm-sum): Shapley by 2-D DP, core membership by sorting; "the certificate costs exponentially more" (citing Conitzer–Sandholm 2006, Ballester 2004) is false for the river and the citations do not cover it. Where something can be out-computed it is information ($h_i$, outside option), which is $L$. Settle by: an afternoon's table timing DP, split and sampler at $n\in\{50,10^3,10^4\}$.

**C10.** The formula assumes $2^n$ enumeration. With the DP, $\bar n^*\propto p^{-1/2}$ to $p^{-1/3}$; with $\epsilon$-play by sampling, $\propto p^{-1}$. At today's $p$ the DP gives Krier's $10^4$ residents exactly for \$5–\$500, the sampler to $10^{-4}$ for cents. Node 3's "bargain small?" is yes at every scale Krier names; leaf C is unreachable through compute. What unpinning restarts is Cooter's haggling (plan 12(e) already saw this). The point that survives, without #P: exact play is affordable at every realistic size, so the division is contestable only strategically, which is why T-ii rests on proposal power alone.

**Objects.** $V(c)=v\Phi(\Delta\sqrt c/\sigma)$ is one decision with a known gap. Over a population of decisions with gaps dense near zero the aggregate loss is $\sim\sigma^2/(4c)$, a power law, no knee. $\sigma$ and $D$ are algorithm properties, and algorithms have historically fallen as fast as hardware (Bixby 2002) [sure]; "hardware never touches $D$" is true, "nothing touches it" false. For LLMs, inference-compute returns are log-linear with no knee (Brown et al. 2024; Snell et al. 2024) [sure]. The dichotomy omits: information about the state (Radner–Stiglitz 1984 non-concavity), about the opponent (Larson–Sandholm), compute as commitment (Rubinstein 1986), instance selection, coordination. "Once truth-telling is dominant nothing is left for compute to buy" is wrong: under VCG agents still acquire information about their own values, and VCG induces the *efficient* amount (Bergemann–Välimäki 2002) while bargaining induces rent-seeking acquisition (Persico 2000) [sure on existence]. **The right distinction is aligned versus rent-seeking returns, not knee versus prize; that literature is the published form of "point the agents at the rulebook".**

**Verification.** "Polynomial where search was exponential" borrows the NP asymmetry wrongly. Menu membership is P; own payoff at a point is cheap iff the valuation is compactly evaluable, the same condition as C5, so verification and search are cheap *together*; optimality or fairness is the search problem again, and counting has no classical certificate, though sum-check (LFKN 1990) lets a polynomial verifier audit a prover's count if the firm is made to answer as prover, which is a protocol rule. In the convex regime prices are the certificate of efficiency (a node-1 property). What the cap genuinely buys is a *pre-evaluated* contract, a resident substituting the rule-maker's evaluation for her own; the public good is a public exposure model, which nobody inside the bargain supplies, a better argument for T-iv than the one the essay runs. C15 is right for the wrong reason.

**Worst case.** The factory controls $m$ and which offers, not valuation structure, weights or $n$; hardness for the reader needs entangled reader preferences the drafter cannot supply. The real drafting advantage is amortization and shrouding (Gabaix–Laibson 2006) [sure], which run on reader attention, the one input LLM reading makes cheap: **the drafting weapon is the thing cheap compute disarms, inverting C6–C7.** Planted hardness needs a trapdoor and is detectable, so a reader with a veto can refuse anything outside a tractable class, a unilateral cap needing no outside enforcement, narrowing T-iv to $k<n$. Computational social choice precedent: manipulation is easy on every natural distribution (Conitzer–Sandholm 2006; Procaccia–Rosenschein 2007; Friedgut–Kalai–Nisan 2008; Isaksson–Kindler–Mossel 2012) [sure]. Legitimate worst-case use is the mechanism *guarantee*, but Krier claims no guarantee and neither regulation nor the firm offers one.

**Outside the list.** Treewidth sentence still over-quantifies (Courcelle gives non-elementary $f(t)$, Frick–Grohe 2004) and is decoration, since the river's $v$ is a sum. Dan's draft line "$C=\mathcal O(e^m)$ exponential in the number of configurations" reads as $2^{2^m}$. The $2^m$ human-elicitation count should be the compact representation's parameter count, amortized; new *kinds* of harm cost attention, not new clauses. Citations at the core certificate do not cover the river. Snyder–Ting–Ansolabehere 2005 [likely]: in Baron–Ferejohn under weighted voting, payoffs are proportional to weights, public and $O(n)$ to read, so $2^n$ is attached to a solution concept nobody plays. The one-model rule cuts the other way: a hand-solvable model has tractable solution concepts.

**Most damaging.** *The complexity bin is empty on the essay's own model, by the argument the plan accepted for $B_n$.* Every exponential is a worst case over an unstructured class the river's definitions exclude. What is left is the floor and the strategic uses of drafting and proposal power. "The gap between cheap and free compute is where the utopia leaks out" carries nothing, because the leak is at free. The stronger essay: computational transaction costs are the ones that genuinely fall to zero; Krier's pitch solves the part of the problem that was not the problem; standard forms and pinned divisions are needed for strategic reasons (Tirole 2009; PSS 2008; Bergemann–Välimäki 2002) that a lower $p$ does not touch. That version drops the exact-play band, the pivotality race, "#P in cents" and the clause blow-up, and keeps every verdict.

## Economist (Opus), received fourth

Results marked † were checked against an abstract or survey in-session. "Example 1" (cited for C13) and "Example 2" (for C14) exist in no file; two targets cite evidence nobody can check.

**C9 and W1. FAILS as applied.** True as a conditional (fixed prize, constant-elasticity technology), but the mechanism it is attached to violates the hidden premise that the prize is independent of compute. From the essay's own primitives, resident error $s_r=\sigma/\sqrt{c_r}$, firm error $s_f=\sigma/\sqrt{c_f}$, offers to residents the firm's signal says are underpricing: expected transfer per resident $=V s_r^2/\sqrt{2\pi(s_r^2+s_f^2)} = (V\sigma/\sqrt{2\pi})\,c_r^{-1/2}(1+c_r/c_f)^{-1/2}$, checked by Monte Carlo. Consequences: the win probability $\tfrac12+\arcsin\rho/\pi$, $\rho=(1+c_r/c_f)^{-1/2}$, is ratio-form (degree 0), so the "ratio of error bars" intuition is right about *who wins*; the prize is the defender's own error, degree $-1/2$ in the defender's compute, so with money fixed the transfer falls as $\sqrt p$ and with endogenous spending transfer and spend both fall as $p^{1/3}$ (at equal stakes the resident outspends the firm, $c_f/c_r\approx0.41$); compute beyond parity buys the firm almost nothing (71% of cap at parity, 99.5% at 100×), so the defender faces a decision problem with a knee, contradicting the draft's "the knee never rescues us" and "a bigger shovel against an exponential mountain" (additive sampling error is polynomial: keeping the pick-off under 10% of a fair share at $n=10^6$ is about $10^{13}$ operations). What survives is a pick-off that shrinks with $p$, or a data asymmetry (Snow's inferred minimum), which is information structure and belongs in the floor. *General rule:* with $P_A=f(c_A)/(f(c_A)+f(c_B))$, uniform cheapening is neutral iff the elasticity of $f$ is constant (Tullock power family, Skaperdas 1996†), sharpens for the richer side iff it rises (logit/difference, Hirshleifer 1989), equalizes iff it falls (saturation, the knee, C10). The ratio form is a knife-edge, not a default. Difference form applies to additive performance with fixed noise (Lazear–Rosen), races to a fixed target (Che–Gale 2000† → all-pay; HFT latency), and volume contests against a fixed-capacity reader. In agent bargaining the credible sharpening case is the clause menu, drafting linear in $m$ against pricing $2^m$, which is leaf B, not leaf C. *Internal inconsistency, the clearest sign of confirmation bias:* Tullock with a fixed prize burns $v\,r(n-1)/n$ in money whatever $p$ is, so under C9 the draft's "cheaper compute lets both sides burn more of the surplus", "the loss growing as $p$ falls", "dissipation is $L$ growing as $p$ falls", the plan's vocabulary row, and T-iii are all false; rising dissipation needs difference-form behaviour (full dissipation in the all-pay limit, Baye–Kovenock–de Vries 1996), the form C9 rejects to keep the share fixed. **No contest success function gives an invariant share and rising dissipation at once.** The only consistent route is the extensive margin (more disputes cross a participation threshold), never argued. Hirshleifer's paradox of power (1991) also cuts against the essay. *Budgets:* the model has no wealth variable, so "fixed money budgets" is not expressible; the right primitive is stakes, under which interior spending is free of $p$ in money and the prize divides by the ratio of stakes, and the firm's stake spans $n$ residents while each resident's spans one, which is Olson in contest form; even an $n$-fold stake does not rescue the pick-off. Effective prices are not uniform (provider price discrimination; "six months newer" is algorithm access, not FLOP price). *Narrowing:* restate C9 for prize-independent, constant-elasticity contests, say the pick-off is not one, and move sharpening to the clause menu; delete "dissipation grows as $p$ falls" or rest it on the extensive margin. Che–Gale 1998: a bid cap can raise total spending. *Settle by:* simulate the weighted voting pick-off with both budgets scaled together (model predicts $\sqrt p$); tournament of LLM negotiators with both inference budgets multiplied by the same factor.

**W2. HOLDS WITH NARROWING.** Direction right in the stationary symmetric case; limit wrong. With recognition probabilities $\rho_i$, stationary ex ante payoffs equal $\rho_i$ at every $\delta$ and the proposer premium vanishes as $\delta\to1$ (Eraslan 2002); with heterogeneous patience, shares $\propto\rho_i/r_i$ (Binmore–Rubinstein–Wolinsky 1986), so Krier's poor farmer loses at any speed. Machine speed turns proposal power into recognition probability and equalizes only if recognition and patience are already symmetric. Counting matters: per-head recognition gives the residents $n/(n+1)$, a club bargaining as one gets $1/2$. Majority closed-rule proposer keeps $(n+1)/(2n)$, persists. Non-stationary multiplicity bites exactly at $\delta\to1$ (Shaked's three-player any-partition for $\delta\ge1/2$†; Baron–Ferejohn Prop. 2†), so equalization is an equilibrium-selection claim, not a theorem; exit options restore uniqueness (Krishna–Serrano 1996). *Commitment changes the limit:* Ellingsen–Miettinen 2008† (cheap binding commitment: all surplus to one side or high conflict probability); Abreu–Gul 2000 (war of attrition settled by commitment priors and patience, protocol-independent). **The machine-speed limit is a commitment race, not equal division.** *Pro-Krier results omitted:* the Coase conjecture (Gul–Sonnenschein–Wilson 1986: an uninformed proposer who cannot commit loses power as offers become frequent), which undercuts C13's corner at machine speed unless the firm can commit; Banks–Duggan 2000/2006 Thm 10† (patient players converge to the core for scalar policy, so setter power evaporates for $k$ or $\bar m$); Diermeier–Fong 2011† (reconsideration limits a persistent setter). *Narrowing:* faster counter-offers move the share toward a split weighted by recognition and patience; under one-sided private information power shifts to the informed side unless the proposer commits; recognition, patience and the commitment race decide which way. A larger point for Krier than W2 concedes.

**C13. FAILS as an invariance.** The triple underdetermines the share: a determinate model needs recognition probabilities, the amendment rule, commitment or re-proposal, delay cost and patience, the information structure, an equilibrium selection. "None depends on $p$" is true only because delay cost and commitment were left out. $\mathcal W$ is itself a constitutional choice; $R_0$ is endogenous once rules reopen. "Leaves everyone at reservation" holds only under complete information; with private $h_i$ take-it-or-leave-it is monopsony screening, residents keep information rents, some efficient deals fail, and the rents shrink as the proposer's inference improves, so $p$ moves $S$ toward the proposer: the draft's own price-to-quote drift contradicts T-ii. "Verification pins $X_j=0$" holds only for a veto exercised after the draw; for a regime adopted ex ante, unanimity secures only interim IR and $X_j$ can be positive. TIOLI for adhesion contracts: right as protocol (Kessler 1943; Rakoff 1983), wrong as $p$-invariant: TIOLI is itself an economy on negotiation cost, so $\mathcal P$ depends on $p$; $R_0$ is the best rival offer, set by search costs (Diamond 1971; Stahl 1989); the informed-minority mechanism (Schwartz–Wilde 1979; Bakos–Marotta-Wurgler–Trossen 2014†: one or two per thousand read) means agents could make everyone a reader unless terms are personalized; platforms move $R_0$ after lock-in. *Conflict with C8:* Snyder–Ting–Ansolabehere 2005†, in Baron–Ferejohn with weighted voting expected payoffs are proportional to weight, not to power indices; if the protocol sets the share, Shapley is not what anyone bargains to, "a reservation price is a fact about pivotality" fails, and leaf C loses its precondition. The essay must pick one. *W11:* Krier's club uses take-it-or-leave-it aggregation; if the club makes the offer, the corner hands the surplus to the residents. *Narrowing:* holding the protocol fixed, the share is set by the protocol, not $p$; then concede cheap cognition moves each element and say for whom.

**C3 and W3. FAILS as stated.** It contradicts C1: $k=1$ is the substitutes case and has the empty core with welfare $-4$, so "substitutes → leaf A, Krier right" is false for the model's first example, and "clears efficient" is wrong in the welfare sense. Jordan and Kelso–Crawford are private-goods results; each consent harms non-signers (Segal 1999). Node 1 must ask "substitutes *and* no externality on non-traders", after which leaf A contains none of Krier's externality cases and the concession is made on uncontested ground. Collusion in substitute markets: Calvano et al. 2020; Klein 2021; Fish–Gonczarowski–Shorrer 2024†; Assad–Clark–Ershov–Xu 2024 JPE† (German gasoline, margins rise only when all stations adopt algorithmic pricing); Brown–MacKay 2023 (faster repricing raises prices through commitment, no collusion needed, a speed contest at leaf A); Banchio–Skrzypacz 2022† (format matters); counter, Asker–Fershtman–Pakes 2022†. The mechanism is the folk theorem: cheaper monitoring raises effective $\delta$, the same $\delta\to1$ W2 credits to Krier; the essay cannot take one and refuse the other. *It cuts for Krier in the river:* the $k=1$ race is Bertrand among consent sellers; in the repeated consent game collective refusal with grim reversion is subgame-perfect iff $\delta\ge1-h_i/\pi$ for every $i$, which at $h=(2,3,9)$, $\pi=10$ is $\delta\ge0.8$, met by machine-speed repetition; that meets C1's own refutation condition whenever consent is repeated, as plan item 8 already says. Thin markets: price-taking is incentive-compatible only in the large (Hurwicz 1972; Roberts–Postlewaite 1976). The price-to-quote sign depends on market structure (Thisse–Vives 1988; Corts 1998). *Narrowing:* thick private-goods markets, anonymous prices, no externality on non-traders, formats resisting tacit coordination; even there $S$ moves with $p$ through search costs.

**C14 and W7. C14 HOLDS WITH NARROWING; W7's conclusion FAILS.** The category is coherent: Stigler's industry knows its role, not the instances; Buchanan–Tullock's veil of uncertainty is the realistic Rawls. *What the essay misses: Myerson–Satterthwaite is an interim theorem.* With participation fixed before types are drawn, the expected-externality mechanism (d'Aspremont–Gérard-Varet 1979; Arrow 1979, on the property-rights doctrine itself) is efficient, budget-balanced, Bayesian incentive-compatible and ex ante individually rational, so parties who know their roles but not the instances can unanimously adopt an efficient rule, and what remains is a lump-sum split of a known gain, which complete-information bargaining settles. "Contest over roles" is a fight over a transfer; dissipative only if it is influence-buying rather than bargaining. **Role-ignorance buys agreement on the division, not efficiency.** So the veil branch is where the preamble's floor lifts, the opposite of decorative; its cost is interim losers, $X_j>0$ for some types; **T-i and T-iii trade off along the M–S frontier, and leaf E's "cheated less and efficiency gains" is a frontier, not a free pair.** One role-committed designer is agenda control, not a contest; a contest needs several organized lobbies (Becker 1983; Grossman–Helpman 1994). Role-ignorance is rare for one-sided persistent harms, common for reciprocal rotating ones (traffic, nuisance, default rules, procedure) and durable general rules, and can be manufactured (generality requirements, lead times, pre-siting rules, issue bundling). Capture is not the default (Carpenter–Moss 2013†; Levine–Forrence 1990†); the models between veil and capture (Peltzman 1976; Becker 1983; Grossman–Helpman 1994, where offsetting contributions give efficient policy when every group is organized; Laffont–Tirole 1991) each run on a variable cheap cognition moves, plausibly toward the diffuse side; against, Olson's problem is incentives, not cost. *Narrowing:* ex ante rule-making escapes the interim floor and role-ignorance removes the dispute over the division; where roles are persistent, rule-making is a contest among organized interests weighted by organization cost, salience and information.

**Outside the list.** C1 is overbroad: M–S is interim and entitlement-dependent (Cramton–Gibbons–Klemperer 1987; Ayres–Talley 1995; Segal–Whinston 2011); Mailath–Postlewaite does hold at large $n$; "floor invariant in $p$" fails at node 4 under ex ante timing; the $k=1$ empty core is one-shot. ## Cooperative game theorist (Fable), received fifth

Verified by LP and enumeration in the scratchpad: the $k=1$ core is empty at $\pi=10$ and at $\pi=20$, and in general for $\pi>H$ at every $n\ge2$ (for $\pi<H$ nonempty iff $\pi\le\min_i h_i$, and then $\{0\}$); Shapley at $k=1,\pi=10$ is $(\phi_f,\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3)=(21/4,7/12,-1/12,-23/4)$; $v$ is superadditive at every $k$ and non-monotone at every $k<n$; the chain-restricted core at $k=1,\pi=10$ is nonempty, e.g. $(0,8,-3,-5)$.

**C1.** The emptiness HOLDS under α, β, γ and every partition-function core (Hafalir 2007; Kóczy 2007; Bloch–van den Nouweland 2014), because the binding constraints, $u_f+u_i\ge\pi-h_i$, $\sum_N u\ge0$, $\sum_G u=\max(0,\pi-H)$, are partition-invariant. Chander–Tulkens' game has every player choosing own emissions and no entitlement, which is the river's $k=0$ cell, where the core is already nonempty; it does not reach $k=1$. C–T is constructive at $k=0$ (a named imputation and a converging process), so "the contribution game lands outside the core" is a fact about one naive protocol, and the designed protocol is a rule; the strong form of the $k=0$ failure is Ray–Vohra 2001 (grand coalition fails under equilibrium binding agreements). *The inference "empty core ⇒ Coasean bargaining breaks" HOLDS WITH NARROWING:* Aivazian–Callen assume recontracting; Coase 1981 (in the collection) replied that parties bind themselves; Krier keeps the state as enforcer; under enforceable multilateral agreements the non-cooperative foundations reach the efficient action at $k=1$ regardless of the empty core (Hart–Mas-Colell 1996; Pérez-Castrillo–Wettstein 2001; Macho-Stadler–Pérez-Castrillo–Wettstein 2007 for externalities, which the river needs since $v$ is non-monotone). At $k=1,\pi=10$: no pollution, residents pay the firm $21/4$ net, every party better off than divide-and-conquer. **So the floor at $k=1$ is not the empty core: it is that the protocol must be multilateral with the bilateral exit closed while it runs, and that someone convenes it (Dixit–Olson). Both are rules binding a non-consenter.** *Unification:* the $k=n$ clause is the interim-IR constraint (drop IR and AGV is efficient, incentive-compatible and budget-balanced); the $k=0$ clause is participation; the $k=1$ clause is the bilateral exit. All three say "non-consenters cannot be bound", which is C12. C1 is a corollary of C12, not an independent floor, and its refutation clause "without binding agreements" is a condition Krier never accepts (W11). Side point: the SPE strategies of those bargaining foundations require every player to know the Shapley value of every subgame, so the #P cost sits inside the strategies on both sides.

**Bell cut.** HOLDS WITH NARROWING. α- and β-characteristic functions are superadditive by construction (Aumann 1961), so "superadditive at every $k$" carries no information; in partition-function form superadditivity has non-equivalent definitions and none implies grand-coalition efficiency (Hafalir 2007); on the river the merger $\{f\},\{1\}\to\{f,1\}$ is "superadditive" ($0+0\to8$) and lowers welfare from $0$ to $-4$. The search is vacuous because $W=(\pi-H)x$ depends on $x$ alone, which the plan's Decided entry already says. The residual the cut should not erase: coalition structure matters for *stability* (the equilibrium structure at $k=1$ is $\{f,i\}$ plus singletons; at $k=0$ fragmented: Ray–Vohra 2001; Yi 1997; Bloch 1996), a "which structure forms" question with its own literature, not a Bell-number search.

**C8.** HOLDS WITH NARROWING, and the narrowing changes the mechanism. Shapley–Shubik is a value, an average marginal contribution over orders including those in which the player is not pivotal; nothing in it is a floor. The cooperative reservations are the disagreement payoff, the core lower bound, the nucleolus or kernel, or a Nash payoff from a threat point; the essay uses the core for C1 and Shapley for C8 without saying why the concept changes, and in the Coase literature the theorem is read as core nonemptiness with the division indeterminate inside it. In the $(1,1,3)$, quota-4 game the core is $\{(0,0,1)\}$, as are the nucleolus and kernel: residents 1 and 2 have an enforceable floor of zero and Shapley hands them $1/6$ each they have no coalitional power to keep. "The firm picks off residents who underprice themselves" is the firm moving the outcome from the Shapley yardstick toward the core vertex, taking what the entitlement never enforced: an equity complaint against the yardstick, not a stability or efficiency failure. *Computation:* for weighted voting games core nonemptiness and membership are polynomial (veto check; Elkind–Goldberg–Goldberg–Wooldridge 2007/2009), the nucleolus pseudo-polynomial (Elkind–Pasechnik 2009), Shapley and Banzhaf #P-complete. So "exact pivotality is #P-hard, which keeps the division contestable" is true under Shapley and false under the core: the precondition for the contest bin is a choice of yardstick. *Private harm:* under any concept where the reservation is harm plus a share, the reservation is dominated by $h_i$, which the resident knows and the firm does not; the informational edge runs to the resident; the pick-off needs the reservation to depend on public combinatorial structure, which contradicts the essay's own private-harm setting. *Selection:* the pick-off is a winner's curse; with common knowledge of both sides' precision the expected selection rent is zero (Milgrom–Weber logic); it survives only under naivety about selection or private firm precision, an unstated bounded-rationality assumption. *Narrowing:* "pivotality is the resident's claim under the Shapley yardstick; the enforceable floor is the core, polynomial in weighted games; what the firm's precision buys is the gap between the two, a transfer the yardstick assigned and the entitlement never enforced."

**C10.** FAILS as the boundary of the contest. For integer weights exact Shapley is $O(\bar n^2q)$, so the band is $\min(2^{\bar n},\bar n^2q)$ and already inside every budget at $\bar n\approx50$; exact play is the wrong target since the pick-off's magnitude depends on $\epsilon$-precision, reached by $O(\log(1/\delta)/\epsilon^2)$ permutations independent of $\bar n$, a knee. *Comparative statics:* with thresholds $\phi_i+\epsilon_i$, $\epsilon_i\sim(0,\sigma^2/c_i)$, firm estimates $\phi_i+\eta_i$, $\eta_i\sim(0,\sigma^2/c_f)$, screening for $k$ cheap consents: extractable rent $O(k\sigma\sqrt{\log(n/k)}/\sqrt{c_i})$ once $c_f\gg c_i$, bounded by the victim's precision; the firm's marginal value of compute reaches zero at finite $c_f$, a knee; under uniform cheapening both spending and extraction fall (free spend: $c^*=(A\sigma/2p)^{2/3}$, loss and money burnt $\propto p^{1/3}$; fixed money: loss $\propto\sqrt p$). Simulation at 50 residents, $k=10$: extraction at $c_i=4$ is $2.9$ at $c_f=64$ and $2.8$ at $c_f=4096$; along the diagonal $c_i=c_f=4,16,64$ it is $1.6,0.8,0.4$. **The pick-off is two decision problems joined by a transfer; leaf C is false both ways; C9's Tullock invariance is applied to a game that is not a contest success function.** *Collapse:* when exact play is affordable the division contest does not collapse: at $k=n,\pi=20$ the core is the whole simplex and exact knowledge of $v$ selects nothing (Cooter's haggling, plan 12(e)); at $k=1$ exact play tells everyone there is no stable division, Aivazian–Callen cycling. *The narrowing that keeps a Tullock contest inside the one-model rule:* the race to recontract in the empty-core cell. At $k=1$ every proposed division is dominated by some $\{f,i\}$; in a dynamic process the firm races to sign one resident before the residents complete a refusal pact; with search and communication rates proportional to compute, $\Pr[\text{first}]=\lambda_f/(\lambda_f+\lambda_N)$, ratio-form, invariant to uniform cheapening, prize fixed by $v(\{f,i\})$ against $v(N)$. A genuine contest about coalition-formation speed (Krier's flash coalitions, the W2 machine-speed point), living where the core is empty, which ties the contest bin to the floor rather than to the #P-hardness of a yardstick. The pick-off then belongs with C6/C7: the firm's reply to residents' improving precision is to enlarge $m$, raising $\sigma$.

**C11.** HOLDS WITH NARROWING. A formula removes the computation of the prize only if enforced against side-deals: $u^\phi$ is coalitionally stable iff $u^\phi\in\mathrm{Core}$. At $k=1$ no formula is stable, so it is pure enforcement; at $k<n$ the firm with any winning coalition improves on any formula that pays non-members, so the formula lives on the no-side-deals rule; at $k=n$ a formula on observables is in the core iff $\phi_i\ge h_i$ for every $i$, an upper envelope that overpays everyone the proxy overestimates, and the overpayment can exceed the surplus (the draft states the underpay side only). *The $k=n$ formula that closes deals is AGV:* transfers as a formula on reports, efficient, incentive-compatible, budget-balanced, violating interim IR, i.e. compulsory participation, the veto removed. **C11 and T-i trade against each other at $k=n$: the formula that closes the deal removes the veto that pins $X_j=0$.** The price of C11 is $X_j>0$ for some types. Where the prize goes: to the constitutional stage, where the concept is self-stability (Barbera–Jackson 2004), under the veil a symmetric formula is self-stable and under role-commitment generically not; and to the observables (Goodhart). Budish coarsens time at no efficiency cost; coarsening the division at $k=n$ costs the deal; the citation is decorative.

**C12.** HOLDS, with counterparts: (a) "binds non-consenters" is the removal of interim IR; M–S and M–P are impossibilities because of IR, AGV the possibility without it; **the deal/rule line is the IR line, and T-iv has a theorem under it.** (b) Jehiel–Moldovanu 1996, strategic nonparticipation; Jehiel–Moldovanu–Stacchetti 1996, a designer who can threaten non-participants extracts from them; Krier's devices are mechanisms with type-dependent outside options. (c) Yi 1997 (in the collection): open-membership games under positive externalities do not sustain the grand coalition; the $k=0$ abatement coalition and the $k=1$ refusal pact both impose positive externalities on outsiders, so one theorem gives both failures. (d) Dixit–Olson 2000. (e) Cartel stability (d'Aspremont et al. 1983): "one refuser sells" is external instability. (f) Buchanan–Tullock 1962; Buchanan 1965's club needs excludability, which abatement lacks. *Correction:* "no new game" is wrong non-cooperatively. A binding club is a commitment technology, raising members' effective $k$ to the club's size and bargaining as one player; what it cannot do is change non-members' options; so it is a new game whose participation stage is the old game, and the participation stage reproduces the failure at both ends.

**Computational core.** Demote. It exists in three forms: cores with restricted cooperation for a fixed family (Myerson 1977; Faigle 1989; Aumann–Drèze 1974; Kaneko–Wooders 1982), which is $\mathrm{Core}(b)$ with $\mathcal F$ fixed, and "coalitions form over the telephone" is Myerson's graph; the ε-core and least core with $\epsilon(S)$ the organizing cost; and Sandholm–Lesser 1997, coalitions among computationally bounded agents, the closest prior art. The budget-endogenous version is ill-posed: $\mathcal F(b)$ needs *whose* budget, discovery is disclosure so $\mathcal F$ is a game outcome, it conflates finding, computing and coordinating, and a stability notion depending on which threats have been computed *yet* is a disequilibrium notion. *A positive theorem the sweep turns up:* restrict cooperation to a tree and the core is nonempty (Le Breton–Owen–Weber 1992; Demange 2004), verified on the river: the chain $f\!-\!1\!-\!2\!-\!3$ at $k=1,\pi=10$ gives $(0,8,-3,-5)$, residents 2 and 3 paying the gatekeeper to refuse. **"Bound the bargain" to a chain is a theorem about core nonemptiness, and which chain is drawn decides who the gatekeeper is, which is the constitutional prize.**

**Other.** C2: Krier's option fee is a liability rule (no consent needed and compensation at self-assessed value owed; Calabresi–Melamed; Posner–Weyl); the model has transfers only by agreement, so a liability rule is inexpressible and "harmed and unpaid" is an artefact; the reimbursing veto does lower $k$; split the claim. C1/C2 at $k=1$ understate: under any multilateral solution the residents *pay* the firm to buy back the entitlement (Shapley $\phi_f=21/4$; Nash from the Segal threat point gives $(11,-1,-2,-8)$); "harmed and unpaid" is the bilateral outcome, the multilateral one is "unharmed and paying", both worse than $k=n$ and both Pareto improvements over divide-and-conquer, which is Krier's counterfactual. "Superadditive at every $k$" is Aumann's convention; $v$ is non-monotone at every $k<n$ (adding resident 3 to $\{f,1\}$ drops the worth from $8$ to $0$). The $k=0$ protocol miss is a theorem (Ray–Vohra) and a designed protocol that lands in the core exists (Chander–Tulkens 1992): fails under naive protocols, succeeds under a rule. T-i: a veto player who knows $h_i$ has $X_j=0$ without verification; $X_j>0$ needs the party to misjudge its own harm, so "through cheap verification" is only the $2^m$ story.

**Most damaging.** The contest bin has no worked mechanism that is a contest. The one the essay builds is two decision problems joined by a transfer: the extractable rent is bounded by the residents' absolute precision, the firm's marginal value of compute reaches zero at finite budget, and uniform cheapening shrinks both the spending and the extraction. Leaf C is false for the mechanism meant to produce it, and C9's invariance is applied to a game that is not a contest success function. Underneath, the quantity whose estimation is #P-hard is not the residents' enforceable floor; the core is polynomial in weighted games and assigns non-veto residents nothing, so "the precondition that keeps the division contestable" is a choice of yardstick, and under the essay's own private-harm setting the informational edge on the reservation runs to the resident. Without repair T-ii and T-iii lose their only compute-dependent example and the thesis reduces to its floor, which is invariant in $p$ and is itself a corollary of C12. The repair available inside the one-model rule is to relocate the contest to the race to recontract in the empty-core cell and to hand the pick-off to C6/C7, where the firm's reply to residents' precision is to enlarge $m$.

Sampling (Castro, cited and dismissed in one clause; error bars shrink below the unit of account as $p$ falls); the DP is polynomial at any fixed weight resolution; compact representations (MC-nets, cited) make Shapley easy; "hyper-granular" does not imply high-order interaction (pairwise over 100 clauses is ~5,000 numbers); the $n$-counts concern the division, which "does not touch $W$", so they cost efficiency only through C3; Nisan–Segal is a private-goods bound and the river is a public choice over $2^m$ configurations, reduction not given; "clause" means permissions at one point (one valuation per deal) and contingencies at another ($2^m$ states), and only the second makes reading expensive. Larson–Sandholm attribution unconfirmed.
