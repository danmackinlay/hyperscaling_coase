The question, verbatim from the mission: "Under which conditions does more computation for bargaining agents produce better outcomes, and for whom?" "Better" is inequality-sensitive. Purpose of the round. The river is the easiest case for the computational optimist: one public action, one private number per resident, money without wealth effects, a closed system, an enforcer assumed, one shot. Every row of the state table was earned there. This round removes those features one at a time and asks whether the rows survive, whether new rows appear, and whether compute at finite budgets favours whoever already holds a position. The essay's spine, if it exists, is the list of conditions the optimist's outcome needs and does not supply; this round is where that list is tested rather than asserted.

The hard-case model, kept small enough to compute by hand. A fleet or factory chooses a schedule x in {0,1}^m over m slots (times and noise types), earning p_t for each slot it uses. Three residents with exposure profiles e_it, public. Resident i's harm is either parametric, h_i(x) = s_i times the sum over slots of e_it x_t, with one private number s_i, or arbitrary, a private value for every one of the 2^m schedules. Transfers; consent rule k of 3; optional remediation, a private good (insulation) that scales a resident's exposure down at a public cost. Switches, each off in the river and on here: arbitrary preferences; recurring schedules, renegotiated each period with discount factor delta; wealth effects, concave utility of money with unequal endowments; no enforcer, so that any pact must be self-enforcing or enforced by a platform that also fixes the clause language; finite budgets, each agent estimating what it needs with error sigma over root c, and a stated ratio of budgets between the sides. Choose m, p_t, e_it and s_i so that the parametric case has a pie comparable to the river's 6, and state them once.

Candidate rows to test, from the river round and the discussion after it. Each is a hypothesis; the round confirms, refutes or narrows it, with status and source.
1. Bits, not operations. With arbitrary preferences the efficient schedule needs communication exponential in m (Nisan and Segal), which no budget of operations buys; with parametric preferences it is m per-slot comparisons on public inputs. Real noise preferences are parametric enough: a judgement to test, not a result.
2. The contract language is a rule somebody fixes. The practical remedy for 1 is a restricted clause language or standard form; who fixes it (the platform, by default) decides whose preferences are expressible and is therefore a party to the bargain.
3. The pact stage inherits the entitlement. Once residents know their exposure, proposing a pact or mechanism reveals type (Myerson's informed principal); durable mechanisms (Holmstrom and Myerson) are defined relative to the status quo, which is the entitlement, and can be as unequal as the status quo allows. Under a veil, before exposure is known, the expected-externality mechanism is unanimously acceptable; the veil is available for recurring small disputes with standing instructions and not for one-off large ones.
4. The enforcer regress ends outside bargaining. Who chooses the enforcer is itself strategic; the regress stops only at a sovereign (Cooter's Hobbes theorem; Acemoglu's political Coase theorem) or at a self-enforcing convention of the repeated game (Hurwicz's guardians; Greif; Milgrom, North and Weingast); compute's role at that level is lock-in of whichever convention formed first.
5. Finite budgets pay for distance from the optimum, and the bounded side pays. Exploitability, the gap between a bounded strategy and its best response, is extracted by whoever can compute the best response, at a rate that shrinks with the bounded side's own budget; complexity-cost equilibria differ from unbounded ones; the cell-B deal-rate curve is the river instance. State each row's effect at finite budgets beside its limit.
6. Scale worsens the incentive problem where the veil is thinnest (Mailath and Postlewaite), and outside options in realistic cases discipline holdout and favour whoever holds them.
7. Wealth effects break the separation of efficiency from distribution (Petel's poor farmer; the essay's own aside): the efficient schedule depends on who pays.
8. The incumbent reading: in every row where compute moves the split, it moves it toward whoever already holds the entitlement, the single principal's seat, the better agent, the first convention, or the rigid stance. One human, one principal, one agent (Petel) is a precondition, since a side can otherwise be inflated.
9. Bundling of deals is an agent-side commitment device (Snow's bike lane): the cost of reopening a bundle is a revocation cost the principal did not choose.
