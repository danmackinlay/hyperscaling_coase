## What the post is

A critique of Seb Krier's "Coasean Bargaining at Scale" and the Shahidi et al. "Coasean singularity" thesis: AI agents slash bargaining transaction costs, therefore private deals displace regulation. The frame: the syllogism equivocates on "transaction costs" (Dahlman resource / Cooter strategic / computational-communication), and the gap between *cheap* and *free* compute is where the utopia leaks out. Anchored to ONE model (the consent-game river) and two size parameters. Prior-art sweep (2026-08-19) found the full critique unclaimed; nearest neighbours are in Incoming.

The conclusion, as it now stands: cheap cognition pays off roughly in proportion to the un-Coasean structure around it; the small-scale demonstrations will succeed for reasons that do not generalize; at scale without caps the failure is not neutral but extractive; and the constructive move is to point the agents at the rulebook rather than at each other — with the honest caveat that rule design is itself NP-hard and that its hard half was never computational anyway.

**Direction (2026-10-21).** The three-bin thesis failed a red-team on its own model (findings in `hyperscaling_coase_rebuild.md`). The rebuilt thesis: cheap compute converges bargaining to the full-rationality solution of the game the entitlement defines; in adversarial cells that solution is the classical bad one; the cells with good limits are the ones a rule binds; the mechanism is commitment, in four cells (`hyperscaling_coase_commitment_model.md`). **Attacked 2026-10-21 by six reviewers; the standoff cell, the convergence claim, the race and the yardstick failed, and the Krier mapping failed on three devices of six** (`hyperscaling_coase_commitment_model_findings.md`, with a proposed replacement thesis at its top, for Dan's decision). Starting and finishing premises and next steps: `hyperscaling_coase_summary.md`.

## What the targets actually say (primaries read 2026-09-07)

Both read in full. **Krier** (Cosmos Institute, 2025): defines transaction costs as discovery, negotiation and enforcement — Dahlman verbatim; claims massive reduction, *not* zero, and says in so many words that Coase's theorem was a benchmark rather than a description; keeps the state as contract-enforcer and existential-risk backstop ("hybrid system", "framework guarantor"); proposes voluntary institutions built from agents (bargaining club with take-it-or-leave-it aggregation, Harberger option fee, reimbursing veto coalition, flash coalitions, digital unions, hierarchical aggregation at neighbourhood/city level); claims the club is incentive-compatible; claims win-win and "residents are compensated" *and* concedes non-invariance under wealth effects, calling the initial allocation a constitutional choice. **Never mentions the firm.** Scales explicitly: ten thousand residents forming a coalition in a nanosecond, millions of agents exchanging preferences. **Shahidi et al.** (2025, NBER volume chapter): about consumer digital markets — search, shopping, matching, job applications — not externalities or regulation; the *only* Coase they cite is 1937, and they predict "significant shifts in the traditional make-or-buy boundaries" without stating a direction; the phrase "Coasean singularity" appears solely in the title, with a question mark; they treat compute as the agent's binding constraint, expect agent prices to scale with stakes, and expect diminishing returns to agent quality; they separate efficiency from distribution explicitly and make no Pareto claim.

Three hooks from the primaries are placed (2026-09-07): Krier's "constant renegotiation is expensive even for AGI" in the cached-computation section, the disagreement being whether agents *find* the stable point or are *given* it; Krier's incentive-compatibility claim for the club as the second sentence of The computational core, M–S footnoted; Shahidi's compute-as-binding-constraint paragraph in the equity passage of What survives.

## The model (canonical — do not fork)

**Harm is $h_i$, not $\theta_i$** — Dan renamed it 2026-09-07; the post uses $h_i$ throughout.

BigCorp™️'s factory upstream, residents $N=\{1..n\}$ downstream. $x\in\{0,1\}$, profit $\pi x$, harm $h_i x$, utilities $u_f = \pi x - \sum t_i$ and $u_i = t_i - h_i x$ — linear in money, and note there is **no wealth variable**, so budget constraints are not expressible; the model's only departure-from-realism on this axis is diminishing marginal utility, which the setup aside flags via Krier's poor farmer. Efficient action: pollute iff $\pi > \sum_i h_i$.

**Consent rule**: BigCorp may pollute with $\ge k$ of $n$ consents; harm hits everyone regardless ("consent sells permission, not immunity"). $k$ is the entitlement: $k=n$ complements/holdout (Rob, Mailath–Postlewaite), $k=1$ substitutes/divide-and-conquer (Segal; price of a signature → 0 by mutual undercutting, NOT → $h_{\min}$), $k=0$ BigCorp holds the right and residents must fund abatement.

Canonical numbers: $h=(2,3,9)$, $\sum h = 14$; $\pi=10$ (pollution inefficient) and $\pi=20$ (efficient, surplus 6, BigCorp keeps 6 after paying 14). Weighted variant: weights $(1,1,3)$, quota 4 → resident 3 a veto player, Shapley–Shubik $(2/3, 1/6, 1/6)$. Anonymous-price failure at $k=3$: $9 \le p \le 20/3$, empty.

**Characteristic function and cores (2026-10-13; derived in chat, not yet in the post).** Max-min convention, $G=N\cup\{f\}$. For $k\ge1$ and $S\subseteq N$: $v(S)=0$ if $S$ blocks ($|S|>n-k$), else $-\sum_S h_i$; $v(\{f\}\cup S)=\max(0,\pi-\sum_S h_i)$ at quorum, else $0$. At $k=0$: $v(S)=\max(-\pi,-\sum_S h_i)$ and $v(\{f\})=\pi$. In every cell $v(G)=\max(0,\pi-\sum_N h_i)$, the efficient welfare, and **$v$ is superadditive at every $k$**, so $\{G\}$ is welfare-best without search. Cores at $h=(2,3,9)$: $k=1,\pi=10$ **empty** — $u_f=0$ is forced, then $u_i\ge 10-h_i$ sums to $16$ against $0$; the factory with any one resident out-threatens the grand coalition, which is "refuse together, not apart" as a theorem, and an empty core with one firm. $k=3$: the point $0$ at $\pi=10$, the simplex $\{u\ge0,\sum u=6\}$ at $\pi=20$. $k=0,\pi=10$: nonempty, $u_f=10$, residents sum to $-10$ with $u_i\ge-h_i$; $(-1,-2,-7)$ and either single free-ride are in it, double free-riding $(0,0,-10)$ is not, so the collapse is the contribution protocol landing outside a nonempty core. Superadditivity says $\{G\}$ is best, not that it forms; forming is the core (stabilizable) plus the protocol (reached). The three river failures are empty core, protocol miss, and information rent; none is a search failure.

**The $k=0$ material (Dan, 2026-09-07)** is the strategic heart of the model section; the quota-game arithmetic now sits in the core statement above. Under private $h_i$ the strategy set *grows* rather than transforms: refusal survives, and shading is added — graduated where refusal was binary, available to the pivotal as well as the dispensable, and unauditable, because any share-assessing scheme must now *ask*. Hence Mailath–Postlewaite. The entitlement fixes the direction of the misreport, not its existence: understate at $k=0$ to shrink your bill, overstate at $k=n$ to grow your compensation.

**Pivotal-fraction point** (survivor of a cut block, now folded into the dial summary): where the entitlement is divided, BigCorp needs $k$ signatures and no more, so harm outside that $k$ enters no price and the deciding comparison is made against a fraction of the stake. Only the undivided rules ($k=0$, $k=n$) make the buying side beat the whole of it. This reframes the $k=1$ collapse as structural.

$m$ = binary clauses per bargain (worked example: night discharge, summer discharge, odour threshold, skip monitoring). $2^m$ deals; preferences need $2^m$ numbers *because clause values interact*. **Decoupling assumption**: valuations total $n\cdot2^m$, not $2^{mn}$ — flagged in text as friendly; the coupled regime $m(n)$ is named but not modelled.

$u(S) = \pi$ if $|S|\ge k$ else 0 — BigCorp's valuation over signature bundles. At $k=1$ subadditive (substitutes), at $k=n$ superadditive (complements). This is the equation that makes the $k$ dial *be* the substitutes/complements dial. It is a statement about BigCorp's valuation $u$, not about the game's characteristic function $v$ above, which is superadditive at every $k$; do not let the two share the word.

## Coined concepts

- **Computational core** $\mathrm{Core}(b) = \{u : \sum_{i\in S} u_i \ge v(S)\ \forall S \in \mathcal{F}(b)\}$, where $\mathcal{F}(b)$ is the family of coalitions their own prospective members can afford to find. $\mathrm{Core}\subseteq\mathrm{Core}(b)$, swelling as budgets shrink. Kicker: silence measures the search budget, not the absence of a better deal — and the narrow claim is that what breaks is the *inference*, not the consent (Dan's correction; the signatures are valid). **Status 2026-10-13:** the kicker needs no new solution concept, and $\mathcal{F}(b)$ is underspecified (what is affordable, when discovery is itself strategic); candidate for demotion to a sentence in the contest section. Open question 2.
- **Transaction-cost decomposition** $\mathrm{Cost} \ge \sum_a p_a D_a(n,m,k) + L(k,F)$, underbraced price × count and strategic floor. Left side is *what it takes to be sure of the efficient deal*, not the bill on the day; both terms are floors, hence $\ge$ (a worst-case bound says nothing about an easy instance; the M–S floor is zero under common knowledge). $p_a$: Krier's agents lower it, all but the human elicitation price. $D_a$: hardware never touches it, caps lower it via $n,m$. $L$: nothing lowers it but changing the game; the contest bin is $L$ growing as $p$ falls. $k$ sits in both terms. Symbols propagated downstream 2026-09-10. **Do not add a second displayed decomposition**; the Demand sum is the expansion of the first term.
- **Demand decomposition** $\mathrm{Demand}(n,m)\approx n2^m + 2^n$, capacity $n^*(R)$. **$B_n$ removed 2026-10-13** ($v$ superadditive; Sandholm needs a black-box $v$). The $2^n$ is the cost of a *computed* fair division, not of the efficient action, which is a sum; it enters Demand only through the "without systematically expropriating anyone" clause of tolerably-well. Capped: $n2^{\bar m} + 0 = O(n)$. The capacity function was cut 2026-10-13 and the $2^n$ term reframed 2026-10-15 as the cost of a computed fair division, which the red-team then showed costs cents; the entry is historical.
- **Decision problem vs contest** — retired 2026-10-21 with the bins. The knee is an artefact of a single fixed gap; the pick-off is two decision problems joined by a transfer; the right published distinction is aligned vs rent-seeking information acquisition (Bergemann–Välimäki 2002 vs Persico 2000).
- **Institutions as**: commitment devices that choose the cell of the commitment model (2026-10-21): a liability rule is the factory-commits cell written into law, a union or no-side-deals rule is the residents-commit cell; compute armour was this all along; "cached computation" and "contest-enders" are both retired. **Narrowed the same day by the red-team:** the weak form, a rule assigning commitment to one side fixes the division, holds and is Calabresi–Melamed's and Krier's own position; "nothing else gets out of the standoff" fails (sequencing, conditional instructions, priced commitment, a cost on holding out, third-party buy-outs, a change of $k$, a chain restriction, arbitration, repetition); a liability rule changes the entitlement rather than assigning commitment, and a union rule does not bar the factory from committing.

## Things that were corrected — do not reintroduce

- Kaldor–Hicks vs Pareto: they **agree** here. With side payments and quasilinearity, an outcome is Pareto efficient iff its $x$ maximizes $W$; both select the same action and neither picks the division. They part only over *uncompensated* outcomes. (Earlier draft wrongly said "almost nothing here is Pareto-comparable".)
- The compensation test **is** the surplus test under quasilinearity; the "could compensate" phrasing is the general definition, not an extra condition.
- Pinning the division costs **allocatively**, not distributionally, and only when refusals are pivotal — so the cost scales with $k$, and at low $k$ the shortfall is simply borne. A per-capita division *is* an anonymous price, and fails identically.
- The theorems withdraw **two** of the three price properties (anonymity, low dimension); publicity survives the theorem and dies in practice.
- Extraction alone burns no surplus — it is a transfer. The burn is the compute spent extracting, plus breakdowns under private information.
- $B_n$ is not a term of the river's Demand: $v$ is superadditive at every $k$, so the welfare-best structure is $\{G\}$ without search. Do not reintroduce the Bell numbers, the fifty-first-resident arithmetic, or the doubling race. The $2^n$ is the cost of a *computed* fair division under quasilinearity, not of efficiency, which is a sum; nobody pays it, and its job is to keep the division contestable.
- An attacker's claim that under the Shapley division at $k=1$ "every party does better than under divide and conquer" was false (the firm gets $21/4$ against $10$); the Pareto improvement is the even split of the recovered $4$, giving $(11,-1,-2,-8)$. Check every number an attacker supplies against the river before repeating it.
- Theorems about equilibrium play (Coase conjecture, Baron–Ferejohn limits, HMC mechanisms, winner's-curse correction) describe the limit agents approach as budgets rise, not what current agents do; state the bridge claim, never assume it.
- Superadditivity says $\{G\}$ is welfare-best, not that the grand coalition *forms*. Forming is the core (stabilizable at some division) plus the protocol (reaches it). $k=1$ fails the first, $k=0$ the second.
- Under private harms, free-riding is **not** transformed into misreporting — refusal survives intact. Privacy *adds* a graduated, unauditable version available to the pivotal, who could never refuse. (Olson needs no private information.)
- There is no budget constraint in this model, so "a resident can only pay what they have" is not a claim the formalism supports. A ceiling-versus-discount contrast between $k=0$ and $k=n$ smuggles in a second, unstated departure. Under the departure the post *does* name — diminishing marginal utility — the two entitlements distort symmetrically, both toward pollution. The defensible asymmetry is WTA-exceeds-WTP (Krier raises it as the endowment effect), not budgets.
- Krier's poor farmer *sells* rights and is therefore a $k=n$ case, not $k=0$.

- **The commitment model's citation of Ellingsen–Miettinen 2008 and Crawford 1982 (2026-10-21).** Written from the first red-team's paraphrase, unchecked; wrong on both once read. E–M: small positive cost; with never-failing commitment three outcomes survive, two of them one side taking all; the standoff is unique only for success probability $q<1$ and cheap commitment, with probability $q^2$. Crawford: revocation costs are random, private and learned after the attempt; impasse has positive probability; under known costs the game is Nash's demand game with many equilibria. Never adopt a theorem from a reviewer's paraphrase; read it.
- **"Resident 3, who has the most to lose from no deal, breaks first" (2026-10-21).** At $k=3$ no deal is worth $0$ to everyone; the sentence is $k=1$ logic, where resident 1 (bilateral surplus $8$) breaks first and resident 3 ($1$) last.
- **Cell A's "three and three" (2026-10-21).** Four veto players give $1.5$ each, residents $3/4$; "three and three" presupposes the residents' bloc, which the model's own cell C says needs a pact.
- **A hash as a revocation cost (2026-10-21).** Publishing instructions with a hash proves what they say, not that they cannot be replaced; a revocation cost needs a bond, an escrow or a court, which is an institution.
