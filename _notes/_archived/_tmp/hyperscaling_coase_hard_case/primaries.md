## Session reads, 2026-10-22, from Dan's JSTOR copies in `_tmp/hyperscaling_coase_commitment_redteam/`

- Holmström and Myerson 1983 (`4057cbe3…pdf`), read in full. Durability (§7): an incentive-compatible rule δ is durable iff for every alternative mechanism γ there is an "equilibrium rejection" in the voting game where γ replaces δ only by unanimous vote, with the perfectness condition (7.4) that a type who would gain from γ conditional on the others approving must vote for it, and posteriors (7.5–7.6) as trembling-hand limits. So the status quo of durability is the rule itself. Theorem 2: uniformly IC + interim incentive efficient ⇒ durable. Theorem 3: one privately informed individual ⇒ every interim incentive-efficient rule is durable. Theorem 4: durable and incentive-efficient rules exist. §6: an incentive-efficient rule that is not durable; §9: a durable rule that is not incentive efficient. p. 1818: durability against re-contracting after the outcome is a stronger property they do not characterise.
- Mailath and Postlewaite 1990 (`7936c44…pdf`), read in full. Theorem 2 (p. 357): under (i) increasing virtual utility, (ii) densities bounded below, (iii) supports bounded above, (iv) Σ v̲_i + nε < C(n), the maximum probability of provision under IC, ex ante budget balance and interim IR → 0; under (v) C(n) + nδ < Σ E v_i the probability it should be provided → 1. Lemma 2: replica economy, probability decreasing in n. Corollary (p. 358): the per-capita subsidy needed equals per-capita cost minus the average lowest valuation. §5.2 lists the polluting plant with C(n) the plant's net revenue and the disutilities private. Appendix 1: rate n^{-1/4}. Appendix 2: correlated example, common-knowledge efficient, probability → 0 under a bound T on any one tax (and provision possible without the bound).
- Nisan and Segal 2006 (`d0e4f2a…pdf`), read in full. Prop. 1: any efficient protocol reveals supporting personalised nonlinear prices. Prop. 5: general valuations, message dimension ≥ 2^L − 1. Prop. 6: N = 2, approximation ratio > 1/2 needs ≥ C(L, L/2) bits. Prop. 7: N agents, ratio > 1/N needs ln2·exp(L/(2N²) − 2 ln N) bits. Prop. 8–9, Cor. 2: submodular, dimension ≥ C(L, L/2) − 1, no FPAS. Prop. 12: additive or substitute, dimension L, attained by Walrasian per-item prices. Props 13–14: average case exponential for some distributions. §9: L = 40 ⇒ 16 GB, 164 years of typing; no simulation beyond a dozen items. §10: transfers restore incentives without raising the burden much. The instance is a public decision over 2^m alternatives, which is Segal 2007's setting; the four-party bound in row 18 rests on that paper, not this one.
- Segal 2007 (JET 136), gwern.net/doc/economics/mechanism-design/2007-segal.pdf, read through the Jina reader for Corollary 4 (2026-10-22). Verbatim: "When agents have quasilinear utilities in [0,1], the communication cost of approximating the maximum surplus within ε < N − 1 (i.e., achieving a better approximation than by letting one agent choose an allocation) is at least (K − 1) log2(1 + 1/(N − 1)) bits." The proof is a fooling set of N·(N−1)^(K−1) states; the paper notes the corollary reproves Nisan's approximate-disjointness bound and yields Nisan–Segal's combinatorial-auction result. Confirms the bits row's four-party figures.
- Pei 2025, "Commitment, Conflict, and Status Quo in Bargaining", Harry Pei (Northwestern), arXiv:2503.01053, 2 March 2025, read through the Jina reader (2026-10-22). Two players, same δ, commitment to a demand s ∈ [1/2, 1] at cost c, conflict destroys the period's surplus, the status quo is the last conflict-free division. Theorem 1: as δ → 1 and c → 0 there exists a class of symmetric Markov-perfect equilibria, asymptotically efficient and asymptotically renegotiation-proof, in which both commit to 1/2 in almost all periods, constructed by randomising among demanding 1/2, demanding 1 and staying flexible. The paper notes inefficient Markov equilibria coexist (once a player's status quo share is 1, both mix toward committing to 1). Footnote 5: holds for concave u; with linear u the one-shot game already attains efficiency. So: existence, not selection; symmetric players by assumption.
