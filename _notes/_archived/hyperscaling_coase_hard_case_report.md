# The hard case, reported from the state

The hard case tested nine candidate rows on a second instance, a delivery fleet choosing among four time slots against three residents, with the river's pie of $6$ and switches that each remove one of the river's easy features. Every row was computed twice, independently.

## The answer

Off the river as on it, more compute for bargaining agents raises the Nash product for everyone only where the rules already fix a symmetric limit; wherever one of those rules is missing, compute moves the split toward the holder of the offer seat, a dial, the outside option or the form, and the cost lands on the resident who is most harmed, poorest and least standard at once.

## Rows that moved

Eleven river rows changed off the river; rows 5, 7, 8, 11, 13 and 17 did not.
Row 1's hump returns under the fleet's offer.
Row 2, monotone on the river, now peaks at a middling budget.
Row 3 gains a finite-budget result: a proposer drawn at random by party climbs toward the equal split with nobody losing, while one drawn by side gives $(3;\,1,1,1)$.
Row 4's exception does not reproduce and is flagged river-only.
Row 6's fixed-order number transfers to the instance without a re-run.
Row 9's patience bound constrains the fleet's own reneging, not a residents' pact.
Rows 10 and 15 extend to two consents of three: the core is empty, every resident ends below no deal, and the fleet's compute lowers the floor further.
Row 12's assessor error repeats on the instance, and row 14 stays uncomputable.
Row 16 narrowed: the impossibility at scale needs overlapping supports, and estimation moves the rents to the fleet.

Rows 18 to 28 are new.
All nine candidates were narrowed rather than confirmed or refuted, seven with both computations agreeing.
Row 26 restates the incumbent reading: compute moves the split toward whoever holds the seat or the dial, not whoever holds the entitlement.

## Where the existence test stands

The null fails off the river too.
It fails on rows 20, 21, 23 and 24 under any reading, and on row 25 when harm is a utility loss.
It survives on rows 18, 19, 22, 26 and 27 only under the conditions below.
The spine of two lists, the symmetries the agenda presupposes and the rules that repair the rows symmetry cannot, is now computed on two models.
Argued as hard as it goes for the foil, the case concedes that Krier's devices supply these conditions, each with a distributional parameter that compute then sharpens.

## What the equal split needs

The optimist's outcome, $1.5$ each, was reached only with all of these in place.

- A veto for every resident, with someone outside the bargain setting the consent rule (row 21).
- A proposer drawn at random by party, or symmetric Nash bargaining as the convention (rows 22, 26).
- Transfers personalised to each resident (row 19).
- A clause language and preference model fine enough to carry every party's harm (rows 18, 23).
- Harms verified or common knowledge (row 20).
- No outside option held by one side (row 24).
- Linear money, or a stated choice of units (row 25).
- One human, one principal, one agent (row 26).

Two conditions carry a positive sign, in the foil's favour.
An agent that carries a full table of a resident's preferences, rather than one number, reaches the just schedule under arbitrary preferences, which no scalar agent reaches at any budget (row 18).
An agent that searches schedule and remediation together finds a larger pie that no slot-by-slot bargain finds, and every party gains (row 28).

## What the state asks you to decide

The criterion's units under wealth effects need a choice among three options, since in utility units the Nash product pays the poorest resident least.
The instance has four defects to settle before the next arm, among them whether harm is a money loss or a disutility, on which row 25 turns.
