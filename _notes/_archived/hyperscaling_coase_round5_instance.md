## How this catalogue is used (revised 2026-10-23)

This is a pool of fully specified arms, not a round. The search runs as `hyperscaling_coase_sampling_brief.md`: twelve runs from dealt starting points, collated by entailment, replicated on the base instance. Arms 2a and 2c are pool D of that brief, the two fixed-instance starts; every other arm is a candidate start a run may be dealt or may reach on its own, with its parameters here as the default a run adopts unless it states and justifies others. Arm 1a was screened by the pilot on 2026-10-23 and is not runnable as written: the sign of the principal's excess over its seat share moves under the learning rate and rescaling, the length of play, the play rule, the feedback and the planner's dynamics approximation, and not under the horizon; see the collation. A redesign must fix expected dynamics, sampled play and full-information feedback, sweep $\eta T/s$ as one convergence parameter, give a learner separate numbers as proposer and responder, and measure compute against the horizon-one take. The "robustness, Dan's rule" item below binds every run.

## The base instance

`hyperscaling_coase_hard_case_instance.md`, called the **base instance** here (that note's word "baseline" means this; the mission's "status quo" is a different thing, defined below). Four slots M, D, E, N with profits $p=(4,\,5.5,\,4,\,5)$; three residents with exposures $e_1=(0.5,0,0.5,1)$, $e_2=(1,1,0.5,0.5)$, $e_3=(0.5,0.5,1,2)$ and sensitivities $s=(1,1,2)$; harm $h_i(x)=s_i\sum_t e_{it}x_t$; efficient schedule M, D, E, pie $6$; consent $k=3$; linear money. The **principal** is the fleet; the residents are 1, 2, 3.

## Common to every arm

- **Scaling with $n$.** An arm with $n=3m$ residents replicates the three exposure profiles $m$ times and multiplies every profit by $m$, so the per-household economy is the one above (the hard case's scale arm did this); harms are not scaled. The $n=3$ case is run with the base sensitivities $(1,1,2)$ and, where the arm draws sensitivities, also under the prior.
- **The prior on sensitivities**, wherever they are drawn: each $s_i$ independently $1$ or $2$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each.
- **Robustness, Dan's rule.** A finding that depends on an incidental parameter is too weak to carry. Every learning arm is rerun at learning rates $0.05$ and $0.2$ beside the stated $0.1$; every arm that draws sensitivities is rerun at the alternative prior $P(2)=\tfrac14$; a finding whose sign changes under either rerun is marked [unsure], reported, and not carried into the state.
- **Criterion.** The mission's: the geometric mean of gains over no deal, as a fraction of its maximum at that $n$, where the maximum is the largest geometric mean over divisions of the realised pie (the equal split under linear money); at finite budgets the geometric mean of expected gains, a failed deal counting as $0$ for every party; where some party has no positive gain, the geometric mean over the parties with positive gains with that number reported; the floor is the worst-off party's gain over no deal; the fraction of residents at or below no deal is reported, the principal's position separately; residents' share and split; efficiency.
- **The status quo**, reported beside the above, resident by resident, never folded into the criterion: the **ordinance** closes N and permits M, D, E with no compensation, so the principal has $13.5$ and the residents $(-1,\,-2.5,\,-4)$ per base triple; every voluntary deal leaves the principal below this, which is not a harm the essay counts. The **liability rule** is reported where the arm can compute it: the principal chooses the schedule maximising profit net of assessed damages, damages assessed at $\hat s_i\,e_i\cdot x$ with $\hat s_i=s_i+\epsilon_i$, $\epsilon_i\sim N(0,0.3^2)$, reported per draw and in expectation.
- **Information regime defaults**, in the mission's four variables ($O_h$ harms observable by the other side; $V_h$ harms verifiable by a court; $O_o$ offers public to all residents; $O_p$ past dealings public): $O_h=0$, $V_h=0$, $O_o=1$ under protocol P (a proposal is to everyone) and $O_o=0$ under protocol T (each resident sees her own offer), $O_p=1$ within an arm's own repeated play and $0$ across arms. Null 3's arms vary these; every other arm runs at the defaults.
- **Protocols.** T: the principal offers a schedule and a payment to each resident; a resident accepts iff her rule says so; a refusal by any resident whose consent is needed means no deal that period. P: each period one of the $n{+}1$ parties is drawn by lot to propose a schedule and payments to every party; each other party accepts or refuses by her rule; a refusal by any party whose consent is needed means no deal that period; seat share $1/(n{+}1)$ of the pie. S: as P with the principal drawn with probability $\tfrac12$ and a resident otherwise; the principal's seat share $\tfrac12$.
- **Agent rules.** Every rule is stated over a resident's *gain*, payment less her own harm under the proposed schedule, on a grid $G=\{-2,-1.75,\dots,4\}$ (step $0.25$). A rule carries one number, her **demand** $d$: as responder she accepts iff gain $\ge d$; as proposer under P she proposes the efficient schedule, demands gain $d$ for herself, and offers every other party an equal share of what remains. L0, fixed: $d=0$ (the hard case's rule). L3, adaptive play with memory $m\in\{5,50\}$: $d$ is the best response to a uniform sample of $m$ of her past periods' offers. L1, mean-based: multiplicative weights over $d\in G$, full-information feedback (each period she scores every $d$ against the offer she received or the response she got), payoffs rescaled to $[0,1]$ by dividing by $8$, learning rate $\eta=0.1$, uniform initial weights, sampled play. L2, no-swap-regret: the Blum–Mansour reduction with L1 as the inner learner. **Strength ordering**, by what the class guarantees against a strategic opponent: L0 $<$ L3 $<$ L1 $<$ L2, each costlier to run than the last; L1 is what a cheap default agent does, L2 what a resident can buy. None is a model of people or of language-model agents; the classes are chosen because the theorems of null 1 are stated for L1 and L2 and because L3 is the state's existing class.
- **The principal's agent** under T and when proposing under P: a policy in the class "schedule $x$ among the sixteen, plus a margin $\mu_i\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2\}$ over its current estimate of each resident's demand", chosen to maximise its expected discounted payoff over a horizon $H\in\{1,10,T\}$ by forward simulation of the residents' *expected* learning dynamics (deterministic, not Monte Carlo) from the current weights. It knows each resident's class and parameters and the exposures; it never sees $s_i$ unless $O_h=1$; it estimates each resident's demand from her acceptance history. $H$ is the compute dial for null 1 (the adaptation dial of the mission). A refused period pays $0$ to everyone.
- **Runs.** Learning arms (1a, 1b, 3b): $200$ runs per cell of $T=500$ periods; reported takes are undiscounted averages over the last $100$ periods; discounting $\delta=0.95$ enters only the principal's planning. One-shot arms: $20{,}000$ draws per cell. Seeds $1$ and $2$ both reported; a number is carried if the seeds agree to $0.02$ on a gain and $0.005$ on a rate, otherwise the cell is rerun at four times the draws and, if still apart, both are reported and the cell marked [unsure].

## Null 1: compute asymmetry between two sides

**Null** (mission): a compute advantage for one side cannot move the division beyond what the protocol's seats already allow.

- **Arm 1a, the learner and the planner.** Protocol P, $n=3$, $k=3$, $T=500$. Reference cell: every party L0 and the principal at $H=1$, which gives the seat share, $1.5$ each, and is reported first. Then residents in L3 ($m=5,50$), L1, L2, crossed with the principal at $H\in\{1,10,T\}$; and one reversed cell, residents planning jointly as a bloc with equal internal split at $H=10$ against a principal in L1. Report each party's take, the floor, the criterion, the deal rate over the last $100$ periods, and the status-quo comparison. **Refuted** if the principal's take exceeds $1.5$ by more than $0.1$ and rises with $H$ for some class; **narrowed** if L2 holds it within $0.1$ of $1.5$ at $H=T$ while L1 does not.
- **Arm 1b, extortion.** Two players, the principal and the residents as a bloc with equal internal split; each period both demand Half or All of the pie $6$: (Half, Half) gives $3$ each, (All, Half) gives $6$ to the demander of All and $0$ to the other, (All, All) destroys the period. $T=500$. The principal plays the memory-one zero-determinant extortion strategy for this two-action game, which the arm derives from the Press–Dyson linear condition with extortion factor $\chi\in\{2,3\}$ (no mapping from the prisoner's dilemma); the bloc's agent is L0 (always Half), L3 ($m=5$) or L1 over $\{$Half, All$\}$. **Refuted** if the adaptive bloc's long-run take is no lower than the fixed bloc's; the Press–Dyson expectation is that adaptation is what makes the bloc exploitable.
- **Arm 1c, the arms race.** One shot, protocol P with seat probabilities equal to sample fractions (the state's row 3 reading of Young 1998); the residents buy as one bloc with one spend, split equally for the criterion; prices per sample $p_f$ and $p_N$ with $p_f/p_N\in\{1,\tfrac12,\tfrac15\}$; each side chooses its spend to maximise expected take, seat probability times $6$, net of spend. A Tullock contest with the closed form the arm must verify: dissipation $2r/(1+r)^2$ of the pie at price ratio $r$ ($0.5$, $0.44$, $0.28$) and the cheaper side's share $1/(1+r)$. Report spends, split, dissipation, the criterion net of spend, and an individual-buyer variant (each resident spends separately). **Refuted** if the cheaper-compute side's take net of spend does not exceed its seat share at any price ratio; the arm is otherwise descriptive, and its finding is the dissipation.

## Null 2: multi-party adversarial play and the formation stage

**Null** (mission): after the principal announces its offers, the residents' agents can form a pact that resists divide and conquer at a cost that does not grow with their number, with no standing organisation. A **pact** is a vector of minimum gains its signatories commit to refuse below; a **winning coalition** is any set of residents whose consent suffices under $k$; a pact **fails** when the principal can profitably buy a winning coalition out of it.

- **Arm 2a, the formation stage.** $n\in\{3,30\}$ scaled as above, sensitivities private under the prior (the $n=3$ base case also run), $k\in\{n-1,\,n\}$, one shot, protocol T, offers private ($O_o=0$). Timeline. $t=0$: the principal's agent announces a schedule and per-resident payments maximising expected profit under the prior, by Segal's and Winter's ordering (pay the cheapest expected winning set just above its outside option, the rest nothing; the arm enumerates by type counts, not profiles). $t=1$: residents' agents may form a pact by messages, each message to one recipient costing $c\in\{0.01,0.1\}$ to its sender, a pact needing every signatory to have received its terms; pact terms are "refuse below gain $g$" with a common $g$ chosen by the pact's proposer (the first resident drawn) from $G$; the pact is self-enforcing unless the organisation is on. $t=2$: the principal may revise its offers once, seeing which residents signed [Dan: or seeing nothing; the divide-and-conquer literature assumes the principal sees the coalition]. $t=3$: residents respond by their rule (L0 with the pact's $g$ as her demand if she signed). Standing organisation switch: on, a body holding every resident's mandate at $t=0$, which fixes $g$ and binds its members at no message cost, for a fixed cost $F\in\{0.5,2\}$ paid once, split equally; off, no such body. Report, per $n$, $k$, $c$ and switch: whether a pact forms and whether it fails; the principal's take; the floor and the fraction of residents below no deal; total message cost and its per-resident value; the criterion; the status-quo comparison. **Refuted** if, at $k=n-1$, the pact holds at $n=3$ and fails at $n=30$ without the organisation, or the per-resident message cost needed to hold it rises with $n$, and it holds with the organisation; the organisation's cost is reported against the gain it protects. At $k=n$ every pact holds (the hard case's enforcer row), which the arm confirms as its check.
- **Arm 2b, the chain store.** One principal against $M=10$ successive neighbourhoods of the base triple, sensitivities private under the prior, $k=3$; within each neighbourhood up to $R=3$ rounds of offers under T with discount $0.9$ between rounds; the public record ($O_p=1$ across neighbourhoods) shows every past offer and response. Two principal types, tough with prior $\mu\in\{0.05,0.2\}$: tough offers each resident her harm at the prior-mean sensitivity ($1.5$ per unit of exposure) and never raises; normal offers the same at round one and raises to the equal split at round two if refused. Residents are L0 with belief: a resident whose round-one offer is below her harm refuses at round one and accepts at round two whatever is offered above her harm; she never accepts a below-harm offer. Report the principal's take and the residents' positions per neighbourhood over $M$, the share of neighbourhoods where the normal type pools with the tough one, and the comparison with $M=1$. **Refuted** (for null 2's open-system reading) if the residents' average take does not fall with $M$ for the normal type; Fudenberg and Levine's expectation is that it does.
- **Arm 2c, two neighbourhoods competing.** Two base triples with base sensitivities, $k=3$ inside each, protocol T, residents L0; the principal contracts with whichever triple accepts the lower total payment, ties by lot. Status quo: the ordinance in the neighbourhood drawn by lot. Report the clearing payment, each resident's gain and the criterion. The expected result is a clearing payment of $7.5$, every resident's gain $0$, nobody below no deal; the arm is a check, and its finding is that unanimity protects the share of nobody once the principal can choose its counterparty.

## Null 3: information and privacy

**Null** (mission): the agenda's outcome does not depend on what parties can observe or verify about one another beyond their own harms, and information available to everyone weakly improves the outcome.

- **Arm 3a, legibility and the floor.** The hard case's pact arm restated: $n=3$, $k=3$, one shot, sensitivities private under the prior, the principal proposes a mechanism (a schedule and payments as functions of reports) that must be incentive-compatible and leave every type at or above no deal, choosing the one that maximises its expected profit; the symmetric mechanism (equal expected gains) reported beside it. Cells: $O_h\in\{0,1\}$ (at $1$ the principal sees $s$ and the mechanism conditions on it) crossed with $V_h\in\{0,1\}$ (at $1$ a court can enforce payment of true harm, so the mechanism may condition on $s$ even when the principal cannot see it). Report each party's gain at the realised profile $(1,1,2)$, the worst-off party's gain, the low types' rents, the criterion. **Refuted** if any cell with $O_h=1$ or $V_h=1$ lowers the worst-off party's gain, or lowers the gain of the party whose harm became legible below no deal, against the $(0,0)$ cell; the hard case's expectation is that resident 3 gains and the low types lose their rents, which is not a refutation.
- **Arm 3b, the Hirshleifer case.** Arm 1a at $H=10$, residents L1, with $O_h=1$ for the principal (its planner conditions on $s$) against the default $O_h=0$. Report the deal rate over the last $100$ periods and each party's take in both cells. **Refuted** if public harms lower the deal rate or the floor against private ones.
- **Arm 3c, offers in the open.** Arm 2a at $k=n-1$, $n=30$, organisation off, with $O_o\in\{0,1\}$ for the residents at $t=1$ (at $1$ every resident sees every offer before messaging). Report whether the pact forms and holds in each cell and the message cost. **Refuted** if public offers do not raise the chance the pact holds; divide and conquer relies on each resident not seeing the others' offers. ($O_p$ is not varied here: 2a is one shot.)
